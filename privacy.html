01Who we are

Mittar is a companion app built for the global Punjabi community. It is currently operated as an independent project rather than an incorporated company.

For data protection purposes, Mittar is the data controller for the information described on this page. You can reach us at privacy@mittar.ai.

Postal address: 2300 Olympia Dr, Flower Mound, TX 75028, United States

This policy covers the Mittar app and the mittar.ai website.

02What we collect

What you give us directly

  • Your first name, used so Mittar can address you
  • The name you choose for your companion
  • Your language preference, English or Punjabi
  • Your daily check-in time
  • Your region, vibe, and voice settings
  • The messages you send in conversation
  • Any photos you choose to share
  • The moods you select during check-ins
  • Your email address, if you join the waitlist

What is recorded automatically

  • A randomly generated device identifier, created on first launch, which lets Mittar recognize your device across sessions. It is not linked to your name, phone number, or any account elsewhere.
  • Your check-in streak and the dates you opened the app
  • How many messages you have sent on a given day, used to apply daily limits
  • Basic technical information such as device type and browser, plus error reports when something breaks

We do not ask for your phone number, your address, your date of birth, or your location. Mittar does not request location permissions.

03How we use it

We use the information above to:

  • Generate Mittar's replies to you
  • Remember your conversations so you can pick up where you left off
  • Personalize how Mittar speaks, based on the settings you choose
  • Show your check-in streak and mood history
  • Apply daily message limits
  • Fix bugs and improve the app
  • Email you if you asked us to

We do not sell your data. We do not share it with advertisers. We do not build advertising profiles. We do not use your conversations to train AI models.

If you are in the UK or EU, our legal bases are: performing the service you asked for, your consent for marketing email, and our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and secure.

04How your conversations are processed

Mittar's replies are generated by Claude, an AI system built by Anthropic. When you send a message, that message, along with recent conversation history and your settings, is transmitted to Anthropic's API so a reply can be produced.

This means your conversations pass through Anthropic's systems. Anthropic's handling of that data is governed by their own privacy policy and their commercial terms, under which API inputs and outputs are not used to train their models.

Mittar is an AI companion, not a person. It will tell you so if you ask it directly.

05Memory and summaries

Mittar is designed to remember you between conversations. Two things make that work, and both are worth understanding.

First, your recent messages are stored so they can be shown again when you reopen the app and so Mittar has context for what you were last talking about.

Second, once you have exchanged enough messages, an AI-written summary of your conversations is generated and saved. This summary may describe things like your name, what has been going on in your life, who matters to you, and how you prefer to talk. It is included with future messages so Mittar can refer back to things you have told it.

What this means in practice. Personal things you share — about your family, your work, your feelings — may be written into a stored summary and recalled in later conversations.

If you would rather that did not happen, you can delete your data at any time from the About screen, which removes your messages and summaries together. See section 12.

06Photos you share

If you send a photo, it is transmitted to Anthropic's API so Mittar can respond to what is in it. Photos are not permanently stored on our servers and are not used to train AI models.

Mittar only accesses photos you actively choose to send. It does not browse your photo library.

07Where your data is stored

Your profile settings, messages, and conversation summaries are stored in a hosted PostgreSQL database provided by Supabase. The app itself runs on servers provided by Render. Both are located in the United States.

Data is encrypted in transit using TLS and encrypted at rest by our hosting providers.

08Who else touches your data

We use a small number of third-party services to run Mittar. Each one only receives what it needs.

Mittar's daily Hukamnama is retrieved from a public Gurbani API. No personal information is sent when that happens.

09Information stored on your device

Mittar uses your browser's local storage rather than tracking cookies. It holds your device identifier, your settings, your daily message count, and whether you have joined the waitlist.

Clearing your browser data removes all of it. We do not use advertising or analytics cookies, and we do not track you across other websites.

10Email and the waitlist

If you join the waitlist, your email address is stored with Beehiiv and used to tell you when Mittar launches and to send occasional updates about the app.

Every email includes an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing takes effect immediately. We do not sell or rent our email list.

11Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to:

  • Access the personal information we hold about you
  • Correct information that is wrong
  • Delete your information
  • Receive your information in a portable format
  • Object to or restrict certain processing
  • Withdraw consent you previously gave

These rights apply under the UK and EU GDPR, Canada's PIPEDA, and the California Consumer Privacy Act, among other laws. We will never charge you or treat you differently for exercising them.

To make a request, email privacy@mittar.ai. We will respond within 30 days. If you are in the UK or EU and are unhappy with our response, you may complain to your local data protection authority.

12Deleting your data

You can delete everything yourself, without emailing anyone. Open the About screen in Mittar and choose Delete account & data.

This removes your profile, every message, and every stored summary from our database, and clears everything saved on your device. It cannot be undone.

Deleting your app data does not remove you from the email list, since the two are kept separately. To leave the list, use the unsubscribe link in any email or write to us.

13How long we keep things

Your profile, messages, and summaries are kept while you are using Mittar, and are erased when you delete your data. If an account sits completely unused for 24 months, we may delete it.

Email addresses are kept until you unsubscribe. Error logs are kept for a short period and then discarded.

14International transfers

Mittar's servers are in the United States. If you use Mittar from the UK, the EU, Canada, or elsewhere, your information is transferred to and processed in the US.

Where the law requires a transfer mechanism, we rely on the Standard Contractual Clauses included in our agreements with the providers listed in section 8.

15Security

We use TLS encryption for data in transit, encryption at rest through our hosting providers, access controls on the database, and rate limiting to prevent abuse. API credentials are held on the server and are never exposed in the app.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal information, we will notify you and the relevant authorities as required by law.

16Children

Mittar is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, email privacy@mittar.ai and we will delete it.

If you are between 13 and 18, please read this policy with a parent or guardian.

17What Mittar is not

Mittar is a companion app. It is not therapy, not a mental health service, and not a crisis line. It cannot diagnose or treat anything, and it is not a substitute for professional care or for the people in your life.

Mittar does not monitor your conversations for signs of crisis, and nobody is watching your messages waiting to intervene.

If you are struggling, please reach out to someone who can help.
